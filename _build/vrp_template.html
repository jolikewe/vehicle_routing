Get everyone across in two legs, as early as possible.

  1. People wait at two pickup points and each must reach a destination.
  2. Buses drive them to a transfer hub. From there, boats carry them to their destination.
  3. Every vehicle has a few seats and makes a few trips, so someone has to decide who rides which vehicle, on which trip, and when it leaves.
  4. The goal is simple: everyone arrives as early as possible. Below you can change the problem and watch a planner solve it live.

The buses, boats and place names are an illustration. The repo's 18-person instance is synthetic, with travel times drawn at random, and the code calls these things by short labels (see the key).

How the browser planner works

The planner is a heuristic: it searches for good schedules but cannot prove that none is better. Its building blocks are an exact schedule calculator and a search that tries small changes.

The schedule calculator

  • A schedule is just an assignment: each person gets a bus trip, a boat trip and a hub. Trips on one vehicle run in order.
  • Given that, every time is computed at its earliest possible value: a bus leaves its pickup, drives to the hub and drives back empty. A boat drives to the hub empty, waits for its riders, then carries them on.
  • Anything that breaks a rule (too many riders, two hubs in one trip, a boat leaving before its rider has arrived) is rejected.
  • Checked against CP-SAT: this calculator scores the repo's optimal schedule as exactly 531, person by person.

The search

  • Start: a greedy plan that fills each bus and boat in turn and sends every trip to the vehicle that finishes it soonest.
  • Moves: swap two trips, pull a trip earlier or later in one vehicle's order, swap or move people, and (when hubs are free) change a trip's hub.
  • Accept: simulated annealing. Better plans are always kept; slightly worse ones are kept with a probability that shrinks as the search cools, which lets it escape dead ends.
  • Restarts: the search reheats from the best plan, and every fifth round starts from a freshly randomised greedy plan. It runs in a Web Worker so the page stays smooth.

How the browser solver differs from CP-SAT

The rules are the same ones as in planck_optimiser.py: seats per trip, one hub per trip, vehicles start on their home side and return empty, boats only carry people already at the hub, waiting is allowed, and each person takes one bus and one boat through the same hub. The differences:

  1. Heuristic, not exact. CP-SAT proves 531 is optimal for the repo instance. The browser planner only reports the best plan it found, and says how far it is from a proven value only when one is known.
  2. Earliest times. CP-SAT keeps free timing variables (each stop has a waiting variable in 0 to 30 and arrival times are only lower-bounded). The browser schedules every leg as early as the rules allow, which gives the same sum and last-arrival values. For the hub-wait objectives it also holds buses back just in time, which is what CP-SAT's slack allows.
  3. No empty trips. A trip with nobody on board is never planned. In CP-SAT such a trip is allowed but cannot help, and used trips must form a prefix of a vehicle's trips either way.
  4. First launch point. A bus's first trip starts at the pickup of its riders. A boat's first trip starts at whichever destination is closest to the hub it serves. CP-SAT leaves this free too (a documented limitation), so a vehicle effectively starts where it is most useful.
  5. Pickup to destination pairing and hubs. As in the notebook, Pickup North always goes to Isle and Pickup South to Cape. The notebook also fixes each person's hub; the browser keeps that when the box is ticked. With free hubs the browser can beat CP-SAT's 531 because it solves a less restricted problem, so no comparison is shown.
  6. Extra objectives. The notebooks only minimise the sum. The browser also offers last arrival, average hub wait and a weighted mix. The CP-SAT model in the repo was extended to match (next section).
  7. Not modelled in either. A cap on hub waiting (the docs mention 10 minutes) and route choice between nodes: travel times are fixed numbers.

The CP-SAT model, extended

The repo's model has been extended so the formulation's other objectives can be solved exactly, and with the exact answers shown next to the browser planner's. The original behaviour is untouched: the notebook still gives 531.
